# Critical evaluation of the pipeline's methodology

Written as a quantitative-research review of the pipeline's design and known behaviour, not as a dissertation chapter. **The specific numbers quoted below (F1 scores, OOV rates, participation ratios, etc.) come from a prior run of this same codebase, before the three regime-detection sweep scripts' grids were widened from S&P 500/Nikkei 225 only to all four markets** — they illustrate real, reproducible *behaviours* of the still-unchanged modelling code (`modelling.py`, `graph2vec.py`, `eigencentrality.py`, `spectral.py` are all byte-for-byte what produced these numbers), but are not the current full 4-market grid's numbers. The full sweep (all four markets, all epoch lengths, all graph types, plus the embedding-geometry branch) has since been run to completion in this checkout — see [`../COMPILED.md`](../COMPILED.md) for the current, full-scope results this same pipeline now produces.

## What's solid

- **Frozen-model discipline holds up under repeated audit.** Train/test leakage is the single easiest way to invalidate this whole pipeline, and it's been checked for directly: `fit_regime_order` is called on train labels/μ only, `apply_regime_order`/`validate_regime`/`empirical_transitions` all consume a frozen `order`/`crisis_id` rather than re-deriving anything from data they're given. `modelling_walkthrough.ipynb` explicitly asserts `apply_regime_order`'s output matches `rank_of_cluster[labels]` directly, for both K-means and HMM sections, as a standing consistency check.
- **All three descriptor branches share literally the same downstream code.** `cluster_kmeans`, `cluster_hmm`, `fit_regime_order`, `apply_regime_order`, `validate_regime` in `modelling.py` are embedding-agnostic and used unchanged by `modelling_sweep_raw.py` (Graph2Vec), `eigencentrality_sweep_raw.py` (eigenvector centrality), and `spectral_sweep_raw.py` (spectral/RMT). This is a real strength for the parsimony-benchmark argument: any difference in their results is attributable to the descriptor, not to divergent clustering/scoring logic.
- **The eigenvector-centrality benchmark is a genuine complexity-vs-performance test, not window dressing.** It's deterministic, has zero fitted hyperparameters, and its one interpretability feature (`argmax_id`) is a real, checkable claim, not a hand-wave — though see below for how far that claim holds up.
- **The VGAE dead-end was investigated and abandoned honestly**, rather than quietly dropped — it's documented (see [`architecture/pipeline.md`](architecture/pipeline.md)) as the reason there's no 4th learned-embedding branch.
- **Sweep scripts are resilient**: per-combo `try/except` with a failure list, incremental CSV writes after every successful combo — a crash partway through a run doesn't lose already-computed rows. `embedding_geometry_sweep.py` extends this with a merge-by-key write (`_write_results`) so a scoped `--arms` re-run only replaces the rows it actually reproduces.

## Open methodological risks

### 1. Historical full-grid results: Graph2Vec modestly outperforms eigenvector centrality on average, but per-combo variance is large

On a prior S&P 500/Nikkei 225-only run: mean SP500 test F1 was Graph2Vec K-means 0.272, HMM 0.228; eigenvector centrality K-means 0.197, HMM 0.138 (means across that run's SP500 combos). On average, Graph2Vec modestly outperformed eigenvector centrality on both clustering methods — but on a single illustrative combo (`SP500/raw/132d/mst`), centrality's K-means F1 (0.408) beat Graph2Vec's (0.305), the opposite ranking. Both statements are true simultaneously: the aggregate favoured Graph2Vec, but per-combo variance is large enough that any one combo can go either way — evidence for, not against, finding #3 below's "don't trust a single combo's F1" point. Given finding #5 (Graph2Vec's test-time embedding is structurally noisier than centrality's), Graph2Vec still averaging a higher F1 despite that handicap is a mild point *in favour* of the parsimony argument being real — though this deserves a proper significance test (paired across combos, or a Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the F1 differences) rather than a bare mean, which has not been done on any grid, historical or current.

### 2. The localisation gate isn't robust to skew — and it clustered in one arm

`eigencentrality_sweep_raw.py` flags a combo when `mean_pr < 10`. On a historical grid that also covered the `mg`/`gr` arms, 9 combos had `median_pr < 10` (severely localised in the typical window) but the mean-based gate only caught 4 of them. The pattern was not random: the missed combos were concentrated in `mg`-arm MST networks across multiple markets and timescales. A reader trusting `pr_localised_flag == False` on any of those combos would be trusting a number that doesn't reflect the typical window. The gate should use `median_pr`, or report the fraction of windows below 10, rather than (or in addition to) `mean_pr` — this is unchanged in the current `eigencentrality.py`/`eigencentrality_sweep_raw.py`.

### 3. F1 on small, imbalanced held-out crisis samples is highly unstable

Directly demonstrated: comparing Graph2Vec's and eigenvector centrality's K-means regime timelines on `SP500/raw/132d/mst`, the two descriptors *agreed* on 141/164 (86%) of test windows — visually near-identical — yet Graph2Vec's F1 (0.305) was substantially below centrality's (0.408). The entire gap traced to the 23 disagreement windows (14% of the test set), against a base rate of only 16 actual "VIX > 30" windows out of 164. With that few positive examples, a handful of boundary-window misclassifications is enough to swing F1 by 0.1 or more. **Any single combo's F1 should be read as noisy, not as a precise estimate** — this is a structural property of validating against a rare event on a modest-sized test set, not a bug to fix, and applies identically to the current 4-market grid (all four markets have similarly-sized test sets; only S&P 500 gets an F1 at all).

### 4. Graph2Vec's own training vocabulary is already sparse, not just a generalisation problem

A historical `SP500/raw/63d/threshold` sweep row showed `oov_rate_train_mean = 0.41` — on average, **41% of a training window's own WL-hash features aren't frequent enough to survive `min_count=5`** and enter the Doc2Vec vocabulary, before test data is even considered (`oov_rate_test_mean = 0.48`, only modestly worse). This means even the *in-sample* embeddings are built from a vocabulary that misses close to half of each graph's own structural fingerprint. Worth investigating directly rather than assuming the fixed hyperparameters (`wl_iterations=3`, `min_count=5`, `dimensions=128`) are well-matched to this problem's actual feature cardinality — this has not been tested.

### 5. The two methods' held-out embeddings are not equally faithful — a real asymmetry, not a nitpick

`eigencentrality`'s `transform_centrality_embed` is an exact linear projection (`scaler.transform` → `pca.transform`); Graph2Vec's `transform_embed` calls Doc2Vec's `infer_vector`, an approximate, iterative re-optimisation against a fixed vocabulary. Combined with finding #4, Graph2Vec's test-set embeddings carry materially more approximation noise than centrality's. Any head-to-head comparison of the two methods' *test-set* performance should state this explicitly rather than presenting it as a level playing field — it structurally is not one, independent of which method is "better."

### 6. HMM's conditional-independence assumption is violated by window overlap (documented, still open)

All three epoch configs (`378/22`, `132/10`, `63/5`) give ~92–94% overlap between adjacent windows, so consecutive embeddings are far more autocorrelated than `GaussianHMM`'s per-state emission independence assumes. Combined with `cluster_hmm`'s train-log-likelihood-only seed selection, a seed can be chosen because it fits train's redundant, overlap-driven structure well, not because it captures genuine transferable dynamics. Consequence: a small `regime_persistence_gap` (train vs. test) currently can't be told apart from "the same overlap-driven self-transition inflation present on both sides of the split." Flagged at design time, not addressed since. HMM dwell times / self-transition probabilities have not been cross-checked against a de-overlapped (e.g. one-window-per-non-overlapping-block) baseline anywhere in the codebase.

### 7. PCA's sample-to-dimension ratio is tight in several real combos

Train is roughly 75–80% of the full sample, already modest for the 378d epoch. On historical data: `SP500/raw/378d/mst` (eigenvector centrality) retained `pca_dims = 39` from `n_train_windows = 201` — a ratio of about 1 retained dimension per 5 training samples. `SP500/mg/63d/mst` retained `pca_dims = 237` from `n_train_windows = 944` for the raw-indexed 350-dimensional descriptor — a much richer retained dimensionality than the same market's other arms, worth checking against overfitting risk specifically, not assumed benign because the raw sample count is large.

### 8. Eigenvector centrality's "interpretability" claim is weaker than it sounds

The one concrete advantage claimed for eigenvector centrality over Graph2Vec is `argmax_id` — naming the single most-central stock. Historical full-grid data showed this answer is genuinely unstable: across SP500's combos, `modal_argmax_id` took 8 distinct values; across Nikkei225's, 7 distinct values. Neither market had a single dominant answer even within itself. The interpretability claim should be framed as "this descriptor can always name a plausible, currently-central stock," not "this descriptor identifies THE systemically important stock."

### 9. A single, fixed train/test split has never been stress-tested

The entire causal-split design rests on one cutoff (`2019-12-31`). The test period that results from it happens to contain both COVID and the 2022 rate-shock — arguably the two most dramatic, "easy to detect" volatility episodes of the whole sample. No sensitivity check exists for whether apparent test-set performance would hold up under a different cutoff, or whether it's substantially propped up by these two outlier episodes specifically. This is worth at least one robustness check (e.g. a second cutoff a year or two earlier/later) before treating current test-set numbers as representative of "how well this generalises," rather than "how well this detects two of the most obvious crises in financial history."

### 10. Only S&P 500 has external validation — the other three markets' "universality" claims rest entirely on internal structure

By design (VIX is S&P-500-specific), `validate_regime` is only ever called for `index_code == "SP500"` across all three regime-detection sweep scripts. Whatever cross-market "universality" argument the dissertation makes rests, for Nikkei 225/FTSE 350/CSI 300, entirely on `empirical_transitions`' train-vs-test structural comparison — i.e. "does the regime-transition structure look similar," never "does the detected crisis regime actually correspond to a real, externally-verified stress episode in that market." This is an honest, deliberate scope decision, but it should be stated plainly wherever cross-market results are presented, not left implicit. It also means widening the sweep grid from 2 to 4 markets (see [`architecture/pipeline.md`](architecture/pipeline.md)) added breadth of structural comparison, not breadth of validated results — FTSE 350 and CSI 300 are in exactly the same "no external validation" position Nikkei 225 was in.

### 11. `ARM_THRESHOLDS` is calibrated on one market and applied to all four (new since the market-scope widening)

`networks.ARM_THRESHOLDS = {"raw": 0.65}` is derived in `threshold_diagnostics.ipynb` from a pooled 5%-exceedance target on **S&P 500 at T=132 only** (see [`math/graph-filtering.md`](math/graph-filtering.md)), then applied uniformly across all four markets and all three epoch lengths in `data_processing.py`. This gap existed even at 2-market scope but is now wider: Nikkei 225, FTSE 350, and CSI 300 all have structurally different baseline correlation levels, sector concentration, and market-mode strength than S&P 500 (a smaller or larger dominant-eigenvalue gap changes what "5% of pairs exceed this value" means in absolute correlation terms), and none of that is checked. A stock market with systematically higher baseline pairwise correlation than the S&P 500 would see threshold graphs built with an effectively looser cutoff than intended (more edges survive), and vice versa for a lower-correlation market — this could bias graph density, and therefore every downstream descriptor, differently across markets in a way the current pipeline has no mechanism to detect or correct for.

### 12. Run-to-run non-determinism is real and was directly observed, not theoretical

`gensim`'s multi-threaded Doc2Vec training is only approximately reproducible even with every seed fixed at 42. Two independent reproductions of the identical `SP500/raw/132d/mst` K-means combo produced 43 vs. 44 predicted-crisis test windows respectively (a one-window difference), changing the reported F1 in the third decimal place. Small, but real — any reported Graph2Vec number should be understood as "this run," not as an exactly reproducible constant.

## Recommendations, roughly in priority order

1. **Fix or supplement the `pr_localised_flag` gate** to use `median_pr` (or report the below-10 window fraction) rather than `mean_pr` alone.
2. ~~Investigate the `mg`+`MST` localisation pattern directly~~ — moot: the `mg`/`gr` correlation transforms were removed from `networks.py` (the analysis only ever depended on `raw`), so this pattern can no longer be re-examined without reinstating them.
3. **Run the sweep grids against the current 4-market scope** to get fresh numbers — everything quantitative above predates the widening from 2 to 4 markets.
4. **Run a proper significance test on the full-grid F1 comparison** (e.g. paired/Wilcoxon across combos) before stating "Graph2Vec outperforms eigenvector centrality" or vice versa as a conclusion.
5. **Re-derive (or re-validate) `ARM_THRESHOLDS` per market**, or explicitly test whether the S&P-500-derived thresholds produce comparable graph density across all four markets, before treating cross-market comparisons as apples-to-apples (see finding #11).
6. **Treat single-combo F1 numbers as noisy estimates**, not precise results — report a spread (e.g. across HMM seeds, or a handful of nearby cutoffs) rather than a single 3-decimal number.
7. **Investigate Graph2Vec's vocabulary hyperparameters directly** — the 40%+ in-sample OOV rate suggests the current `min_count`/`wl_iterations` may be a poor match for this problem's feature cardinality.
8. **State the test-time fidelity asymmetry explicitly** wherever Graph2Vec and eigenvector centrality's held-out performance is compared side by side.
9. **Run at least one alternate train/test cutoff** as a robustness check before relying on the current split's test-set numbers as "the" generalisation result.
10. HMM window-overlap/independence (#6) and PCA sample:dimension ratio (#7) remain open and un-addressed.
