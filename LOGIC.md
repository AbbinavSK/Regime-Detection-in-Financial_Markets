# LOGIC.md — the reasoning behind this pipeline

This is not a code walkthrough — `docs/math/*.md` already cover formulas function-by-function, and the code is short enough to read directly. This document answers a different question: **why is the pipeline shaped the way it is, what is each piece actually testing, and how do the pieces fit together as one research design rather than four unrelated scripts?** It's written for a reader with a stats/ML background who has *not* read this codebase — assume familiarity with PCA, eigenvectors, and HMMs, not with this project's files.

---

## 1. The question underneath the question

The dissertation's surface question is "can regimes be detected from correlation-network topology instead of price statistics." Underneath that sits a sharper, more falsifiable one: **when you build an expensive, learned representation (Graph2Vec) and a cheap, deterministic one (eigenvector centrality, spectral/RMT), does the expensive one actually buy you anything?** Everything about how this pipeline is structured — the shared downstream code, the two independent "control" branches — exists to make that question answerable rather than rhetorical. A pipeline that only ever ran Graph2Vec could never answer it, no matter how good Graph2Vec's numbers looked.

## 2. The shared substrate

All three descriptor branches sit on top of the same rolling-window correlation machinery, and it's worth understanding this layer first because every downstream comparison depends on it being genuinely shared, not re-implemented per branch.

**Rolling windows.** A window of `epoch_length` trading days of log returns produces one Pearson correlation matrix; the window slides forward `epoch_shift` days and repeats. Three `epoch_length`s (63/132/378 days ≈ one quarter / half-year / eighteen months) give three timescales of "how much history defines a regime." Windows overlap heavily by construction — at `epoch_length=132, epoch_shift=10`, consecutive windows share 122 of 132 days — which is cheap to compute but has a real statistical cost that resurfaces below (HMM independence).

**Markets.** Four markets are acquired and network-constructed: S&P 500, Nikkei 225, FTSE 350, CSI 300. The three regime-detection branches (§4-6) sweep all four. VIX validation (§2, "VIX, and its limits" below) is S&P-500-only, so only S&P 500's detected crisis regime is ever scored against an independent, externally-verified stress signal — the other three markets' results are internally-consistent structural comparisons, not validated detections.

**Arms.** The pipeline builds correlation networks from the `raw`, unmodified correlation matrix only — no assumptions about what's "noise" versus "signal" baked in before the descriptor even sees the data. Two other lenses, `mg` (RMT-denoised: bulk eigenvalues clipped to their mean) and `gr` (market-mode-removed: the dominant eigenvalue/eigenvector subtracted out), were implemented and swept early in the project, but no regime-detection branch ever depended on either, so both were removed from `networks.py`/`data_processing.py` — the pipeline now only ever computes and pickles `raw`.

The single remaining graph-construction threshold (`networks.ARM_THRESHOLDS = {"raw": 0.65}`) was derived once in `threshold_diagnostics.ipynb`, from S&P 500 at T=132 only, then applied to all four markets and all three epoch lengths — a real, still-open external-validity gap now that the pipeline covers four structurally different markets rather than the two it was originally calibrated against (see [`docs/math/graph-filtering.md`](docs/math/graph-filtering.md) and [`docs/critical-evaluation.md`](docs/critical-evaluation.md#11-arm_thresholds-is-calibrated-on-one-market-and-applied-to-all-four-new-since-the-market-scope-widening)).

**The causal split.** Every branch fits everything — descriptor, scaler, PCA, clustering — on windows ending on or before 2019-12-31, then only ever *applies* those frozen fits to later windows. This isn't a generic ML best-practice reflex; it's load-bearing for the whole premise. If clustering saw test-period correlation structure while fitting, "detects the 2020 crash" would partly mean "was shown the 2020 crash," and the VIX validation number would be measuring memorisation, not detection.

**μ as the regime key.** `mu_offdiag(C)` — the mean off-diagonal correlation of a window — is the one number every branch agrees is a reasonable proxy for "how crisis-like is this window," because it's the textbook stylised fact this whole project is built on: correlations that are normally dispersed compress toward 1 when everything sells off together. Every clustering method (K-means, HMM) produces *arbitrary* cluster labels — cluster "0" has no inherent meaning and can flip between runs (the standard label-switching problem). `fit_regime_order` breaks that arbitrariness once, using only train labels and train μ: whichever cluster has the highest mean μ *is* "Crisis," full stop, and that mapping is frozen and reused for test. This single design choice is what makes it meaningful to later say "Graph2Vec's Crisis regime" and "eigenvector centrality's Crisis regime" in the same sentence — without it, comparing F1 across descriptors would be comparing differently-defined labels that happen to share a colour in a plot.

**VIX, and its limits.** The detected "Crisis" regime is scored against real VIX spikes (VIX > 30) for the S&P 500 only — no equivalent series has been sourced for Nikkei 225, FTSE 350, or CSI 300. This means every claim about "how well this generalises across markets" currently rests on structural comparison (does the *shape* of the regime-transition behaviour look similar across markets), never on a non-S&P-500 market detecting a real, externally-verified stress episode. Worth holding onto this limitation from the start, since it recurs under every branch below.

## 3. Why three descriptors, not one — the two-axis design

A single new descriptor, run once, would only ever produce one number to argue about. This pipeline instead varies two independent axes and keeps everything else fixed, so a difference in outcomes can be attributed to *one specific thing* changing:

|                                   | **Input: filtered graph** | **Input: raw correlation matrix**                   |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Descriptor: learned**     | Graph2Vec                       | *(not built — would need a graph-free learned method)* |
| **Descriptor: handcrafted** | Eigenvector centrality          | Spectral/RMT                                              |

- **Graph2Vec vs. eigenvector centrality** holds the *input* fixed (both read the same MST/TMFG graph) and varies the *descriptor* — this is the direct test of "does a 128-dimensional learned embedding beat a single deterministic eigenvector, given the same graph to work from?"
- **Eigenvector centrality vs. spectral/RMT** holds the *descriptor style* fixed (both are handcrafted, both are literally "take an eigenvector of something") and varies the *input* — this is the test of "how much does throwing away everything except a sparse graph backbone actually cost?" A correlation matrix has roughly $N(N-1)/2$ pairwise entries; an MST retains exactly $N-1$ edges. That's not a rounding error, it's the overwhelming majority of the pairwise structure discarded before eigenvector centrality ever runs.
- **Graph2Vec vs. spectral/RMT** differs on *both* axes at once, which is exactly why it isn't run as a standalone headline comparison — a difference here can't be attributed to either axis alone without the other two branches as anchors. This is the corner-to-corner comparison the 2×2 design exists to make interpretable rather than ambiguous.

Eigenvector centrality is doing double duty as the pivot for both axes — which is precisely why it needs to be the most defensible, least-hyperparameter method in the whole comparison (see §5).

## 4. Branch 1 — Graph2Vec: learning what a graph's *shape* looks like

The problem Graph2Vec solves: a graph is a variable-size, unordered structure with no natural fixed-length vector representation, but K-means/HMM need one fixed-length vector per window. Graph2Vec treats "embed a graph" as "embed a document": Weisfeiler-Lehman relabeling turns each node into a hash of its $k$-hop neighbourhood, pooled into a bag-of-words fed to Doc2Vec (PV-DBOW). Nodes are anonymised to plain integers before any of this (`prepare_graph`) — a deliberate choice to capture *shape*, not *identity*: two structurally-identical networks built from completely different stock universes would embed near-identically. This is the exact opposite choice eigenvector centrality makes (§5), and that contrast is the whole point of running both. Full mechanism: [`docs/math/graph2vec.md`](docs/math/graph2vec.md).

**Fit vs. infer is not a stylistic detail.** A train window's embedding is a direct *byproduct of training* — it falls out of Doc2Vec's parameters once fitting converges. A test window's embedding is a *separate optimisation problem*: `infer_vector` re-runs gradient descent against the frozen, already-trained vocabulary, and any WL-feature that never made the training vocabulary cut (`min_count=5`) is silently dropped rather than approximated. On real data this isn't a small effect — a historical sweep row showed **41% of a training window's own features already miss the vocabulary cut, rising to 48% at test time**. That asymmetry matters the moment Graph2Vec's F1 is compared against eigenvector centrality's — see §7 for why it doesn't invalidate the comparison, just qualifies it.

**Reproducibility.** Even with the WL hash pinned to a seed-independent `zlib.crc32` and every explicit seed fixed at 42, `gensim`'s multi-threaded Doc2Vec training is only *approximately* reproducible — thread-scheduling noise in Hogwild-style SGD means two runs of the identical combo can produce a one-window difference in predicted-crisis test windows, moving reported F1 in the third decimal place. This was observed directly, not assumed. Quoting a Graph2Vec F1 to three decimals should be read as "this run," not as a fixed constant of the method.

## 5. Branch 2 — Eigenvector centrality: the classical, zero-hyperparameter baseline

Eigenvector centrality's definition is a fixed point, not an algorithm: a node's importance is proportional to the sum of its neighbours' importances ($v = \lambda^{-1} A v$) — nothing to fit, nothing to seed, nothing to tune, which is the entire point of including this branch: it's the null hypothesis against which Graph2Vec's complexity has to earn its keep. Full definition, the connectivity requirement (Perron-Frobenius forces the plain `threshold` graph out of this branch's grid — measured directly: plain threshold graphs cover only 43% of the stock universe on average, fragmenting into ~158 separate components in a typical window), the binary-not-distance-weighted adjacency choice (and the VGAE collapse it was chosen to avoid repeating), and the localisation pathology on tree-like MSTs: [`docs/math/eigencentrality.md`](docs/math/eigencentrality.md).

**One weak point worth restating, since it recurs in §10:** the pipeline's current localisation gate (`mean_pr < 10`) catches only a minority of severely-localised combos on a full grid, because a handful of well-behaved windows can pull a combo's *mean* participation ratio above the threshold while its *typical* window stays badly localised — it clustered almost entirely in the `mg` arm's MST networks (an arm since removed from the pipeline — see "Arms" above). **`mean_pr < 10` passing should not be read as "this combo is fine."**

**Identity vs. shape — a genuine, but overstated, interpretability edge.** Unlike Graph2Vec, `window_centrality` keeps node identity throughout (`argmax_id` names the single most-central stock in a window) — the opposite design choice from §4. That claim is weaker in practice than it sounds: historically, across the S&P 500's raw-arm combos, the *modal* most-central stock took 8 different values; Nikkei 225 showed 7. Read this as "this descriptor can always name a plausible, currently-central stock," not "this descriptor has identified *the* systemically important one."

## 6. Branch 3 — Spectral/RMT: reading the correlation matrix without ever building a graph

Every graph-construction step — a threshold, an MST backbone, a TMFG triangulation — is a *choice* about what correlation structure to keep and what to throw away. This branch exists to ask what happens if you don't make that choice at all: eigendecompose the correlation matrix itself, directly, and read off regime structure from the spectrum — market-mode level/dispersion, beyond-market eigenvalue shares, a Marchenko-Pastur bulk-breadth count, bulk entropy, and two participation-ratio echoes of §5's diagnostic (`pr_corr_v1`/`pr_corr_v2`, on the correlation matrix's own leading and second eigenvectors rather than a graph's — the same descriptor style, applied on the other side of the §3 input axis, not code reuse for its own sake). Full RMT framing and the current 9-feature set (cut to 8 from 14 in a redundancy audit, `pr_corr_v1` since reinstated): [`docs/math/spectral.md`](docs/math/spectral.md).

**A singularity worth restating for what it illustrates about the audit discipline (§10 item 1 has the fix):** a sample correlation matrix built from $T$ observations over $N$ names has rank at most $\min(N, T-1)$, not $\min(N,T)$ — Pearson correlation demeans each column, costing one degree of freedom. At $T=132, N=350$ (S&P 500), 219 of 350 eigenvalues are structurally zero, not small. An earlier version of this code used $\min(N,T)$, one off, silently folding a spurious near-zero eigenvalue into noise-bulk statistics for every rank-deficient window — caught by instrumenting the actual computation across thousands of affected windows, not by re-reading the formula. This is exactly the failure mode RMT framing exists to avoid — "measuring the zeros, not the market" — and it's why every eigenvalue-counting step in this branch is done by *position* in the sorted spectrum, never by a magnitude tolerance that could quietly drift.

**No learned component at all.** `np.linalg.eigh` on a fixed matrix is exactly reproducible — same input, same output, on any machine, every time. This is the sharpest possible contrast with Graph2Vec (§4) and even slightly sharper than eigenvector centrality (§5's empirically-verified-but-not-guaranteed determinism) — there's no iterative solver here to depend on a starting point.

## 7. Why comparing across branches is fair, not apples to oranges

The reason it's meaningful to write a sentence like "Graph2Vec's K-means F1 modestly beats eigenvector centrality's" is that, past the point where each branch produces its own $X$ matrix, **every subsequent step is the exact same function call**, not merely similar code: `cluster_kmeans`, `cluster_hmm`, `fit_regime_order`, `apply_regime_order`, `validate_regime` all live in `modelling.py` and are embedding-agnostic — they operate purely on `(X, labels, dates)`, with no branch-specific logic anywhere inside them. All three sweep scripts call them identically. This is what turns "which descriptor is better" from a vague impression into a controlled comparison: any difference in outcomes is attributable to what went into $X$, not to divergent scoring logic.

That guarantee makes the *scoring* fair, not the *inputs* automatically fair — §4's fit-vs-infer asymmetry means Graph2Vec's held-out $X$ carries materially more approximation noise than eigenvector centrality's. It doesn't make the comparison meaningless, but the fact that Graph2Vec still averages a higher full-grid F1 *despite* that handicap (on the historical grid this was checked against) is a mildly more interesting result than the bare number alone suggests — and the reverse is also true: a combo where eigenvector centrality wins is not automatically "centrality is genuinely better here," since Graph2Vec was playing with a structural handicap in how it's tested, not just in what it is.

**The aggregate ranking and any single combo's ranking are different claims.** On one fully-inspected head-to-head combo (`SP500/raw/132d/mst`), Graph2Vec and eigenvector centrality *agreed* on 86% of test windows, yet Graph2Vec's F1 (0.305) was well below centrality's (0.408) — the entire gap traced to 23 disagreement windows out of 164, against a base rate of only 16 true crisis windows. With that few positive examples, a handful of boundary misclassifications swings F1 by 0.1+. Any single combo's F1 is a noisy estimate of that combo, not a precise verdict on the method — this is a structural property of scoring against a rare event on a modest test set, and it's exactly why the pipeline runs a full grid rather than reporting one number.

## 8. Putting the three branches together

| Comparison | What it isolates |
| --- | --- |
| Graph2Vec vs. eigenvector centrality | Does a learned descriptor beat a deterministic one, holding the graph input fixed? |
| Eigenvector centrality vs. spectral/RMT | Does throwing away everything but a sparse graph backbone cost real information, holding the descriptor style fixed? |
| Graph2Vec vs. spectral/RMT | The corner-to-corner difference — only interpretable *through* the two comparisons above, not on its own |

All three are "cluster this descriptor and see how well the resulting Crisis label lines up with VIX (S&P 500) or matches the train-set structure (the other three markets)" — the same downstream machinery (§7), varying only what feeds it, across all four markets. Running all three, rather than just Graph2Vec on its own, is what turns "Graph2Vec gets some F1" from an unanchored number into a claim that can actually be attributed to something: the 2×2 design (§3) means a difference between any two branches can be traced to a specific axis (learned-vs-handcrafted, graph-vs-matrix input), not left as an unexplained gap. See [`docs/critical-evaluation.md`](docs/critical-evaluation.md) for what can currently be concluded from results this pipeline has produced, and what's still open. §9 covers the 4th branch, `embedding_geometry_sweep.py`, which asks a different question about Graph2Vec's embedding shape across network constructions and has its own, much narrower operational scope.

## 9. A fourth lens: embedding geometry across network constructions

§1-8 are all "detect regimes, then score against VIX (or compare structurally)" — one shared question, three descriptors, four markets. `embedding_geometry_sweep.py` asks something else: not *whether* a descriptor detects regimes, but whether Graph2Vec's embedding space's *shape* is stable across the three network constructions §5 builds — threshold, MST-threshold, TMFG-threshold — which are *nested* (threshold $\subset$ MST-threshold $\subset$ TMFG-threshold, each admitting strictly more of the correlation structure than the last). That nesting is what makes this branch useful beyond a curiosity: a shape that survives all three constructions is evidence the regime-detection signal doesn't depend on which backbone the graph-filtering step chose, while a shape that changes markedly between them locates *where* the signal comes from — the same nested-construction logic §4-6's own F1 comparisons could eventually be read against, not just this branch's MDS layouts.

This is deliberately narrower than the three-descriptor comparison of §4-6: only Graph2Vec is used here, not eigencentrality or spectral, and only the raw arm — the only arm the pipeline builds at all (see "Arms" in §2). Restricting to Graph2Vec is what makes including the plain `threshold` construction possible in the first place: eigencentrality's Perron-Frobenius requirement (§5) is the *only* reason any other branch excludes `threshold` graphs, and that requirement doesn't apply once eigencentrality isn't part of the comparison.

This branch's scope is narrow and fixed, not a full grid: `INDEX_CODE="SP500"`, arm fixed to raw, and the distance metric is fixed to cosine with 2-D non-metric MDS only. Two axes are swept — `EPOCH_LENGTHS` (63/132/378, via `--epoch-lengths`) and `GRAPH_TYPES` (threshold/mst/tmfg, via `--graph-types`) — giving nine `(epoch_length, graph_type)` combos, each with its own self-similarity/MDS figure set comparing the three constructions within that epoch length. Extending the remaining fixed axes into a sweep — more markets, a euclidean-distance comparison, 3-D MDS, or other descriptors — is a loop-structure/parameter change to the existing code, not new methodology; it was scoped this way to keep the branch tractable and verifiable. Full scope detail, the output schema, and the stage-3 bundle dependency (now `modelling_sweep_raw.py` only, for all nine combos): [`docs/math/embedding-geometry.md`](docs/math/embedding-geometry.md).

## 10. What this codebase's review history checked, found, and fixed

**Fixed — genuine conceptual bugs, present in the code that produced the numbers cited above, and confirmed still fixed in the current codebase:**

1. **`spectral.py`'s rank calculation** used $\min(N,T)$ instead of $\min(N,T-1)$, silently including one spurious near-zero eigenvalue in every rank-deficient window's noise-bulk statistics. Fixed; verified against thousands of affected windows (§6).
2. **`networks.py`'s `build_arm_networks`** computed `mu_values` (the μ used pipeline-wide for regime ranking, VIX-overlay display, and crisis-cluster identification) from the correlation matrix *before* it was clipped to $[-1,1]$, one line above where the clip actually happens — so on the rare window where a reconstruction pushed an entry marginally outside that range (most visibly on the since-removed `mg`/`gr` arms' eigen-reconstructions, but in principle possible from floating-point error on `raw` too), the reported μ could silently disagree with the same window's graph-construction distance matrix, which does use the clipped value. Fixed by moving the `mu_offdiag` call to after the clip; verified against real data and a synthetic three-arm build that the reordered value now exactly matches `mu_offdiag` of the post-clip matrix. The fix (`mu_offdiag` called after `np.clip`) is still in place and still applies to the raw-only pipeline today.
3. **`spectral.py`'s entropy computation** (`-(p * np.log(p)).sum()`) had no guard against $p=0$, unlike `eigencentrality.py`'s equivalent computation, which explicitly handles it via `np.log(p, where=p>0, out=log_p)`. Fixed to match `eigencentrality.py`'s pattern — confirmed a pure no-op on real windows checked (identical output, no behaviour change) and confirmed not merely cosmetic by constructing a bulk array containing an exact zero: the old code produces `nan` on it, the new code doesn't.

**Checked directly and confirmed safe — no change made:**

4. **`eigencentrality.py`'s `eigsh` call** has no fixed random seed or starting vector — in principle no more reproducible than Graph2Vec's training. Run eight times on a real MST-threshold graph pulled straight from the pipeline, the results agreed to within $6\times10^{-16}$ — floating-point noise, not genuine variation. Expected in hindsight (Lanczos iteration converges to the true dominant eigenvector regardless of starting point, for a well-separated leading eigenvalue), but now a *verified* property. Not because these graphs are trees/bipartite either — checked directly, `mst_threshold_graphs` are **not** trees (they start as an MST backbone but then have every above-threshold correlation edge added on top, which routinely creates triangles and breaks bipartiteness). `eigsh(..., which="LA")` is still the objectively correct choice — it's the direct definition of the Perron root.

**Flagged, not fixed — each is a real behavioural design decision:**

5. `modelling.py`'s `fit_regime_order` ranks clusters by mean $\mu$ via `np.argsort`, which sorts `NaN` last — so a cluster that ends up with zero train members (mean of an empty slice is `NaN`) would be silently ranked as "Crisis" regardless of what it actually contains, and could still receive real test-window assignments even though it was empty on train. Checked every fitted K-means model on disk at the time (36 `.joblib` files, Graph2Vec + eigencentrality + spectral together): none found with an empty train cluster. Strong evidence for real data as it stood, but still not a structural guarantee — the code doesn't prevent this, it just hasn't been observed.
6. ~~`networks.py`'s `arm_correlation` (the `gr` arm) rebuilt the correlation matrix after subtracting the market mode, then renormalised to unit diagonal via `np.sqrt(np.diag(C_arm))` — if the subtraction ever over-corrected a name's own diagonal entry below zero, that `sqrt` would produce `NaN` and silently corrupt that entire row/column's normalisation. Checked all `gr`-arm combos on disk at the time directly for `NaN`: none found.~~ Moot: `arm_correlation` and the `gr` arm were removed from `networks.py` entirely (see "Arms" in §2), so this risk no longer exists in the codebase.
7. `build_arm_networks`'s correlation threshold (`networks.ARM_THRESHOLDS = {"raw": 0.65}`) was derived from a pooled-sparsity target, but **only ever measured against S&P 500 at T=132**, then applied globally to all four markets and all three epoch lengths. This is a live, unresolved gap now that the pipeline covers four structurally different markets — see §2 above and [`docs/critical-evaluation.md`](docs/critical-evaluation.md#11-arm_thresholds-is-calibrated-on-one-market-and-applied-to-all-four-new-since-the-market-scope-widening).
8. Each `data_download_*.ipynb`'s `screen_panel` prolonged-trading-halt screen (`HALT_STREAK_DAYS`) is fixed at 63, uniformly across all four markets — derived from `data_processing.py`'s `EPOCH_CONFIGS`, exactly `min(epoch_length)`, since a flat streak shorter than the shortest rolling-correlation window can never fully saturate any window and produce a `NaN` correlation entry. Verified as zero `NaN`-producing windows across all four markets and all three epoch lengths, using the real `rolling_correlations` call. If `EPOCH_CONFIGS`' minimum `epoch_length` is ever lowered, `HALT_STREAK_DAYS` must be re-derived to match, or the same silent-`NaN` failure mode reopens.

(5) is noted here so it isn't lost, not because it is currently causing a problem; (6) is moot now that the code path it concerned no longer exists; (7) is an open item, not resolved; (8) is a record of a fix that holds as-is unless `EPOCH_CONFIGS` changes.

Everything else checked historically — train/test leakage, the μ-ranking chain being numerically identical across all three branches (same `mu_offdiag` call on the same matrix, every time), `split_train_test`'s order-preservation (which every branch's row-alignment silently depends on), the `K=3`/cutoff/colour constants being byte-identical across all three sweep scripts, and the `graph_type` scoping differences between branches (Graph2Vec includes `threshold`, eigenvector centrality excludes it, spectral has no such axis) — traced back to a specific, correct mathematical reason in every case, not an inconsistency.

What this history did *not* re-litigate: the longer list of open limitations in [`docs/critical-evaluation.md`](docs/critical-evaluation.md) (the localisation gate's mean-vs-median weakness, Graph2Vec's OOV rate, the HMM window-overlap independence violation, PCA's sample-to-dimension ratio in a few combos, the untested sensitivity of results to the single fixed 2019-12-31 cutoff, and three of the four markets lacking external validation). Those are genuine, prioritised, and still open.

## A shelved 4th descriptor: VGAE

A Variational Graph Auto-Encoder (VGAE) embedding method was explored following the same sweep/narrative split as §4-6 but was shelved, then later removed from the working tree entirely. Reason: VGAE collapsed to a degenerate, bit-identical embedding across every window on TMFG-threshold graphs, and was only ever validated on MST-threshold graphs. Confirmed not a KL/posterior-collapse issue; best explanation short of that is TMFG's much higher edge count diluting window-to-window signal through the GCN's neighbor-sum aggregation and weakening the loss's sparsity-reweighting term — not confirmed as the full story. This is why there is no 4th learned-embedding branch alongside Graph2Vec, and why the top-left cell of §3's 2×2 table ("learned descriptor, raw-matrix input") remains unbuilt.
